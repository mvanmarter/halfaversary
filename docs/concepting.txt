I actually think you're approaching this in a good way by starting with a single product rather than trying to build an entire brand at once.

The nice thing about greeting cards is that they're inexpensive to prototype, relatively cheap to ship, and they're a good way to test whether people connect with your core idea before investing heavily. If the underlying concept is strong, it can naturally expand into mugs, shirts, posters, ornaments, and other gifts later.

Here's how I'd think about it.

Phase 1: Validate the concept
Before worrying about inventory or fulfillment, answer one question:

"Will strangers pay money for this concept?"

That means creating perhaps 20–50 card designs around the central idea rather than trying to build hundreds.

Think of it almost like a comedy album or a cartoon series. The concept itself is the product.

Phase 2: Build the simplest possible store
Since I know you've worked with WordPress, WooCommerce, hosting providers, and marketplace plugins before, you're already familiar with much of the technology.

I'd probably build it with:

WordPress
WooCommerce
A clean premium theme
Stripe and PayPal
A blog for SEO
Email signup
This gets you selling quickly and gives you complete ownership.

If you later outgrow it, you can always migrate to another platform.

Phase 3: Don't buy inventory yet
This is probably the biggest piece of advice I'd give.

Instead of printing 2,000 greeting cards and hoping they sell, use print-on-demand for the first several months.

Advantages:

No inventory
No boxes in your garage
No trips to the post office
No money tied up in stock
You only pay when someone orders
Your margins are lower, but you're buying information rather than inventory.

Once one design sells hundreds of copies, then you can order offset-printed inventory and improve margins.

Phase 4: Pick a fulfillment partner
For greeting cards, I'd look at companies that integrate directly with WooCommerce and can print individual cards as orders come in.

Look for:

A2 or A7 folded cards
Envelope included
Ability to print one at a time
White-label shipping
WooCommerce integration
Later, if you move into T-shirts, mugs, and gifts, many of the same companies can handle those too.

Phase 5: Invest in the brand
People rarely remember individual greeting cards.

They remember brands.

Spend time on:

memorable name
great logo
consistent humor or emotional tone
unique voice
You want someone to think:

"Oh, that's one of those cards."

If this were my project...
I'd probably spend a weekend creating what I call a Brand Bible.

It wouldn't have a logo yet.

It would answer questions like:

Who is Halfaversary?
What does it believe?
What emotions should people feel?
What colors feel right?
What colors feel wrong?
What brands feel similar?
What brands feel completely opposite?
What photography style fits?
What illustration style fits?
What words should we always use?
What words should we never use?
Once you have that, designing the logo becomes much easier because you're expressing a well-defined personality instead of starting with a blank page.

One suggestion I think could be especially fun for this project
I would actually create three completely different mood boards, not one.

Warm & Cozy — Think handwritten notes, warm lighting, coffee dates, soft textures, muted colors, intimate moments. This would make Halfaversary feel heartfelt and personal.

Modern & Minimal — Clean typography, generous white space, simple icons, refined packaging, restrained color palette. This would position Halfaversary as a contemporary lifestyle brand.

Playful & Joyful — Bright colors, whimsical illustrations, unexpected typography, confetti, surprise gifts, bold patterns. This would emphasize the delight of celebrating life's little moments.

My guess is that your eventual brand identity will borrow elements from all three, but creating them separately is a great way to discover what truly resonates before blending them into a cohesive visual direction.
